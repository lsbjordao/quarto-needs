6  Architecture decisions

Architecture decisions are first-class engineering objects. They record why a choice was made, which drivers motivated it, where it applies, and how its conformance continues to be confirmed. This treatment follows established architecture practice in which decisions, rationale, and consequences are maintained as explicit engineering knowledge (Bass, Clements, and Kazman 2021), while the document structure remains compatible with lightweight ADR conventions such as MADR (MADR Project 2025).

The canonical type is architecture-decision; ADR is only the identification convention. A decision does not implement a requirement: it addresses it and applies-to architecture elements.

6.1 ADR-001 · Adopt open protocols for federation and provisioning

architecture-decision accepted 2026-08-28

6.1.1 Context and Problem Statement

Consuming applications need to integrate authentication and provisioning without depending on proprietary APIs.

6.1.2 Decision Drivers

  • interoperability across applications;
  • reduced coupling;
  • support from mature libraries;
  • easier auditing and vendor replacement.

6.1.3 Considered Options

  • proprietary APIs;
  • direct integration with the identity database;
  • OpenID Connect for federation and SCIM for provisioning.

6.1.4 Decision Outcome

Adopt OpenID Connect for federation and SCIM for provisioning.

6.1.5 Consequences

The architecture now depends on public, versioned protocol contracts, while reducing coupling between IAM and consuming applications.

6.1.6 Confirmation

OIDC interoperability is confirmed by TC-017; interface contracts are maintained in IF-001 and IF-002.

6.2 ADR-002 · Centralize authorization in a policy engine

architecture-decision accepted 2026-08-28

6.2.1 Context and Problem Statement

Policies implemented separately by each application diverge, hinder auditing, and increase the risk of excessive privilege.

6.2.2 Decision Drivers

  • consistency of access decisions;
  • least privilege by default;
  • explainability;
  • verifiable performance.

6.2.3 Considered Options

  • authorization embedded in each application;
  • distributed rules in shared libraries;
  • a central policy engine with attribute-based evaluation.

6.2.4 Decision Outcome

Centralize the authorization decision in COMP-002, keeping attributes and policies as explicit inputs.

6.2.5 Consequences

The engine becomes a critical component and requires availability and performance targets, but centralizes governance and reduces semantic divergence.

6.2.6 Confirmation

TC-004, TC-005, and TC-006 confirm contextual evaluation, default denial, and performance, respectively.

6.3 ADR-003 · Separate the audit trail from transactional processing

architecture-decision accepted 2026-08-28

6.3.1 Context and Problem Statement

Identity events need to remain intact, queryable, and minimized even when transactional services evolve or fail.

6.3.2 Decision Drivers

  • audit integrity;
  • regulatory retention;
  • personal-data minimization;
  • investigation independent of operational service state.

6.3.3 Considered Options

  • local logs per application;
  • storage together with transactional data;
  • a dedicated event stream to an audit vault.

6.3.4 Decision Outcome

Publish events through IF-003 and store them in COMP-004, with verifiable integrity and a minimization policy.

6.3.5 Consequences

The solution adds an event stream and a specialized persistent component in exchange for greater audit and compliance independence.

6.3.6 Confirmation

TC-010, TC-011, and TC-015 verify integrity, retention, and minimization.

6.4 Lifecycle and lineage

The recommended architecture-decision lifecycle is proposed → accepted, with exits to rejected, deprecated, or superseded. A superseded decision must keep explicit lineage to its replacement through supersedes/superseded-by.
